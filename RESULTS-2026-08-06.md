## Task 1 — block-conditional truth after vectorised training

**Seed 0, 20k steps x batch 256, nfe 32, 500 traces.**

| | well-formed | true |
|---|---|---|
| naive-trained (M4 baseline) | 85.3% | **6.6%** |
| **vectorised** | **95.0%** | **29.4%** |
| unconditional single-block | 95.0% | 38.6% |
| noise floor | 82.6% | 6.7% |

Truth goes from *at the floor* to 4.4x the floor. The naive training algorithm
was the dominant term in the M4 failure.

### Per block — not flat

| block | well-formed | true |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 99.2% | **61.0%** |
| 1 | 99.4% | 47.6% |
| 2 | 96.2% | 29.2% |
| 3 | 96.8% | 34.0% |
| 4 | 93.8% | 19.8% |
| 5 | 91.0% | 8.6% |
| 6 | 88.4% | **5.6%** |

This is the third shape: **decent early, collapsing at b5-b6**. An 11x spread
across block index. So the B=5 data-starvation diagnostic is triggered.

**Two things worth flagging immediately.**

1. **b0 (61.0%) and b1 (47.6%) BEAT the pooled unconditional model (38.6%).**
   b0 is the unconditional task — no prefix — so it should be the unconditional
   model's equal, not its superior. The likely reason is that the unconditional
   model pools every block position into one distribution, and block 0 steps do
   not look like block 5 steps; pooling heterogeneous positions makes the target
   harder, not easier. If so, 38.6% was itself a depressed reference and the real
   comparison is per-position.

2. The per-block **gate** losses after vectorisation are flat (b1-b6 all
   0.56-0.82%) while per-block **truth** spans 5.6-61.0%. That is the third
   independent instance in this project of regression loss and sample quality
   moving differently, and the first where they disagree about *which blocks are
   fine*.

## Task 2 — re-measure, retire, or keep

All measured on the vectorised seed-0 model, nfe 32, against the same numbers
taken on the naive-trained model.

### Retired — these were symptoms of undertraining

| diagnostic | naive-trained | vectorised | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| live-dim variance ratio (gen/real) | 0.831 | **0.992** | gone |
| total variance ratio | 0.837 | **1.000** | gone |
| shell under-norm at t=0 (nfe 50) | -7.8% | — (variance now exact) | gone |

The sampler now reproduces the marginal's second moment essentially exactly.
Both of these were load-bearing in my "the flow cannot hit the target" story and
neither survives. The 1b ablation had already shown the shrinkage was not causal
— decoder truth at the measured 0.912 amplitude was 95.8% against 97.5% — so
this is the second, independent reason to drop it.

Dead-dim ratio moved the other way (1.628 -> 3.887, and +279% relative deviation
at t=0), but the absolute variance there is ~0.0003 against ~1.0 in live dims,
and the 1b ablation showed the decoder ignores those directions entirely: noise
at sd 2.0, four orders of magnitude above what the sampler puts there, left truth
at 97.5%. Noise, not signal.

### Survives — properties of the data or the VAE, not the denoiser

| diagnostic | value | why it is unaffected |
|---|---|---|
| latent clustering, NN/mean | **0.280 vs 0.744 null** (ratio 0.377) | property of the frozen VAE |
| prefix explains 2-7% of next-block variance | unchanged | property of the data |
| unconditional 38.6% | unchanged | had no per-position split to starve |
| shift 1.5 > shift 0.5 by 8pp | unchanged | measured unconditionally |

Re-measured clustering directly and it is bit-identical, as expected.

### Survives but weakened — the trajectory departure

| | naive-trained | vectorised |
|---|---|---|
| departure begins | t = 0.500 | **t = 0.375** |
| max live-dim deviation | 22.6% | **13.2%** |

Still real, still in the mid-to-low-t region where the field has to encode
multimodality, but 40% smaller and starting later. This is now the strongest
surviving structural evidence, and it is genuinely stronger than before *as
evidence* — it was previously confounded with undertraining and no longer is.

### Re-measured: the loss gap — and one "survivor" does NOT survive

Naive-trained vs vectorised, averaged over t:

| block | A (naive) | A (vec) | info (naive) | **info (vec)** | shift (vec) | manifold (vec) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.700 | 0.516 | +0.044 | **+0.204** | -0.004 | +0.017 |
| 2 | 0.722 | 0.472 | +0.112 | **+0.600** | +0.005 | -0.004 |
| 4 | 0.762 | 0.527 | +0.118 | **+0.721** | -0.111 | -0.042 |
| 5 | 0.765 | 0.443 | +0.146 | **+1.298** | -0.219 | -0.028 |
| 6 | 0.826 | 0.488 | +0.163 | **+1.415** | -0.080 | -0.044 |

`info` = cost of swapping the true prefix for another trace's. `shift` = further
cost of using the sampler's generated prefix instead. `manifold` = the sampler's
own output scored as data.

**Three readings, and the first overturns something I listed as surviving.**

1. **"The prefix explains only 2-7% of next-block variance" does NOT survive.**
   Destroying the prefix now costs up to +1.42 against a base loss of 0.49 — a
   4x increase at b6. A signal worth 2-7% of the variance cannot do that. The
   2-7% came from a kNN estimate of Var(z0_b | prefix) in a 64-dimensional prefix
   space, where kNN systematically *under*-estimates predictability, so it was a
   floor artifact of the estimator rather than a fact about the data. The
   naive-trained model genuinely did not use the prefix much (info +0.04 to
   +0.16); the vectorised one uses it heavily. I read an estimator's weakness as
   a property of the problem, and it was the single load-bearing claim behind
   "there is nothing to condition on".

2. **`manifold` has gone to zero** (was -0.07 to -0.17, now -0.04 to +0.02). The
   sampler's own output is now exactly as hard to denoise as real data, i.e. it
   is on the manifold. Consistent with the variance ratio hitting 1.000.

3. **Exposure bias as *distribution shift* still does not exist** — `shift` is
   ~0 or negative at every block. But `info` rises monotonically with block
   index, from +0.20 at b1 to +1.42 at b6. So late blocks depend on their prefix
   FAR more than early ones, and at sampling time their prefix is generated
   content that is worth about as much as an unrelated trace's. That is exposure
   bias in the *informational* sense while being absent in the distributional
   one, and it tracks the per-block truth collapse (b0 61.0% -> b6 5.6%) closely.

This gives a second candidate explanation for the per-block collapse, competing
with data starvation. The B=5 diagnostic separates them: it equalises coverage
but leaves the dependence-on-prefix structure intact.

## Task 6 — SDE vs ODE. This is the result of the night.

Unconditional, nfe 32, on the same baseline model — only the update rule changes.

| eta | well-formed | true |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 95.0% | 38.6% |
| 0.25 | 95.5% | 38.5% |
| 0.50 | 96.4% | 43.2% |
| 0.75 | 97.7% | 50.7% |
| **1.00** | **98.9%** | **70.7%** |

At nfe 8 the same ordering holds but compressed (34.6% -> 44.1%), so the gain
needs steps to materialise — consistent with each step's noise injection being
what corrects accumulated error.

**The eta=0 identity check passes**: max|z_SDE - z_ODE| = 1.6e-05 at nfe 32 and
5.7e-06 at nfe 8. The implementation reproduces the existing Euler sampler
exactly, so the sweep validates itself rather than asserting agreement.

**This confirms the clustering diagnosis and locates the fix on the sampler
side.** A deterministic ODE is a bijection; a strongly clustered target (NN/mean
0.280 vs a 0.744 null) demands a contorted map, and the ODE cannot supply it.
Re-injecting noise every step keeps trajectories where the model was trained.
70.7% against a 74.5% ceiling is 95% of what the decoder can express.

### Jitter — the same hypothesis attacked from the data side, and it fails

sigma chosen at 0.30, the largest isotropic noise keeping decoder truth within
95% of unperturbed (93.1% vs 97.5%).

| model | well-formed | true |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 95.0% | **38.6%** |
| jittered (sd 0.30) | 94.3% | **29.3%** |

Jitter HURTS by 9.3pp. So smoothing the target distribution is not the fix even
though clustering is real. The pair is the interesting part: the same diagnosis
(multimodal target) is correct, but fixing it in the data hurts and fixing it in
the sampler nearly saturates the ceiling.

## Task 4 — prefix-zeroed control. Zeroing the prefix HELPS.

Same architecture, same budget, blocks `< b` zeroed in training and in sampling.

| | aggregate true | b0 | b1 | b2 | b3 | b4 | b5 | b6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| with prefix (mean of 3 seeds) | 29.5% | 61.8 | 48.3 | 30.3 | 33.1 | 20.4 | 7.8 | 4.7 |
| **prefix zeroed** | **33.7%** | 64.2 | 52.8 | 34.2 | 37.8 | 26.4 | 13.8 | 6.6 |

**Better at every single block**, +4.2pp aggregate and +6.0pp at b5. Your
hypothesis holds: the model fits the prefix in a way that does not survive
contact with a generated prefix.

Combined with the loss gap, the ordering is:

  correct prefix (info +1.42 at b6)  >>  NO prefix  >  generated prefix

The prefix is genuinely informative when right and actively harmful when wrong,
and at sampling time it is wrong. That is exposure bias in its purest form, and
it is invisible to the distributional test (`shift` = B - A' is ~0) because a
generated prefix is in-distribution — it is just incorrect.

## The B=5 diagnostic — and a correction to it

**My diagnostic did not do what its docstring claimed.** I asserted that filtering
to traces of <=5 steps makes every block position real in every trace. It does
not: `TraceDataset` drops traces LONGER than B, but shorter traces still pad.
Measured coverage at B=5 was 100/100/100/59.5/21.2%, not uniform. So the run does
not isolate coverage the way it was designed to.

It is still informative, because a valid comparison survives — **matched coverage
across the two configs**:

| block | B=7 coverage | B=7 true | B=5 coverage | B=5 true |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100% | 61.8 | 100% | 60.8 |
| 1 | 100% | 48.3 | 100% | 47.0 |
| 2 | 100% | 30.3 | 100% | 30.4 |

Identical. And the decisive point needs no B=5 run at all: **blocks 0, 1 and 2
all have 100% coverage in B=7, and truth still halves twice** (61.8 -> 48.3 ->
30.3). Data starvation cannot explain the bulk of the per-block gradient.

### Per-block VAE ceiling, measured

| block | coverage | decode true (ceiling) | mean abs(result) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100.0% | 100.0% | 46.6 |
| 2 | 100.0% | 98.4% | 114.4 |
| 4 | 38.9% | 94.4% | 133.7 |
| 6 | 6.0% | 75.9% | 227.9 |

The ceiling does fall with depth, but nowhere near enough: normalising generated
truth by it still leaves a 10x decline (61.8% -> 6.2% of ceiling).

## What actually explains the per-block collapse: OPERAND MAGNITUDE

Binning every generated well-formed step by `max(abs(lhs), abs(rhs))`, ignoring
block position entirely:

| operand magnitude | n | true |
|---|---|---|
| 0-9 | 931 | **58.8%** |
| 10-29 | 4851 | **50.0%** |
| 30-99 | 1209 | **7.0%** |
| 100-299 | 1921 | 3.2% |
| 300+ | 1043 | 2.0% |

**A 7x cliff between the 10-29 and 30-99 bins.** And `operand_max` is 20 — leaf
operands are drawn from 1..20, so values above ~30 only ever appear as
intermediate results. The model has learned arithmetic on the value range it sees
as *inputs* and not outside it.

Holding block position fixed confirms magnitude is doing the work, not depth:

| | 0-9 | 10-29 | 30-99 | 300+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| block 0 only | 69.1% | 61.2% | — | — |
| blocks 4-6 only | 48.3% | 34.4% | 6.4% | 1.4% |

Block 0 never leaves the small-operand bins, which is exactly why it scores 61.8%.
Blocks 4-6 live in the large bins. At *matched* magnitude a block effect remains
(61.2% vs 34.4% in the 10-29 bin) but it is far smaller than the magnitude effect
within either group.

So the per-block decline is mostly a **value-distribution** effect, not a block,
coverage, or depth effect. Deeper steps have bigger numbers; the model is bad at
bigger numbers.

## Decision rule

**Not met as stated, and I think the rule itself needs revising rather than the
work.** Block-conditional (29.5%) does not approach the pooled unconditional
number (38.6%). But three things make that comparison the wrong one:

1. b0 (61.8%) and b1 (48.3%) both *beat* 38.6% decisively across all three seeds.
   The pooled model mixes heterogeneous positions into one target; per-position
   is the honest comparison and the early blocks are ahead.
2. The ceiling is 74.5%, not 97.5%, so the whole scale was wrong.
3. The unconditional number itself is now obsolete as a reference: the same model
   under SDE sampling gives 70.7%, not 38.6%. Comparing an ODE-sampled
   block-conditional model against an ODE-sampled unconditional one holds the
   wrong thing fixed.

**M5 stays blocked**, as instructed, and I have not started it.

## What I would do next

1. **Run the block-conditional model under SDE.** This is the single highest-value
   measurement outstanding and it is one script change. If the eta=1 gain
   transfers (38.6 -> 70.7 unconditionally), block-conditional could move from
   29.5% toward the ceiling, and the entire diagnosis resolves. **Promoting the
   SDE sampler out of §8 is now a real decision with evidence behind it.**
2. **Redo the nfe/schedule sweep** on the vectorised model — the audit's
   outstanding debt. Its "integration error is exonerated" conclusion came from a
   model that could not use its prefix, and the SDE result suggests the sampler
   was the problem all along.
3. **Raise `operand_max`, or train on the value range the model actually meets.**
   The magnitude cliff at ~30 is the largest single effect measured tonight and
   it is a data-design issue, not a model one.
4. Decide what the prefix-zeroed result means for the architecture. A model that
   does better ignoring its prefix is not doing block-causal reasoning.

## What surprised me

- **SDE eta=1 nearly saturating the ceiling** (70.7% of 74.5%). I expected a
  modest gain and got a near-doubling.
- **Jitter and SDE pointing opposite ways** from the same correct diagnosis.
  Multimodality is real; smoothing the data hurts, fixing the sampler works.
- **Zeroing the prefix improving everything.** I expected it to be neutral or
  slightly negative.
- **How much of the M4 diagnosis was undertraining.** Live-dim shrinkage, the
  shell under-norm, the manifold gap — all gone, all things I had built stories
  on.
- **The magnitude cliff.** I spent three sessions on block position and the
  dominant variable was the size of the numbers.

## What to second-guess

- **The claim that 38.6% was "depressed by pooling".** It explains b0 beating it,
  but I have not tested it directly. A per-position unconditional model would
  settle it, and I did not run one.
- **My B=5 diagnostic was wrong about its own premise** and I only caught it
  after the run. The conclusion I drew instead (blocks 0-2 have identical
  coverage) is sound, but it comes from the B=7 data, not from the B=5 run — the
  B=5 run itself added little.
- **The magnitude analysis conditions on well-formedness**, since operands must
  parse to be binned. If malformed output correlates with magnitude, the bins are
  biased. Well-formedness is 95%+ overall so the effect is likely small, but it
  is unmeasured.
- **`sigma = 0.30` for jitter may simply be too large.** I chose it by a stated
  rule, but 0.10-0.15 leaves decode truth untouched (97.6%/97.0%) and might
  smooth the target without damaging it. The negative result is for sd 0.30 only.
- **Every headline number here is on `debug.yaml`** — 2 layers, 128 wide. The
  magnitude cliff in particular could be capacity rather than data.
